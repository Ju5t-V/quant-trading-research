Section 1

Read this first

Every rule in this playbook carries a grade. The grades come from tests run on this feed — XAUUSD M5 from 6 Aug 2019 to 11 Sep 2026 (1,834 session profiles, so 1,833 next-session pairs) for the level rules, and the most recent 2,000 M5 bars (about 8 trading days) for absorption — not from books or videos.

Everything except the candlestick test is in-sample. No period was held back for the level and absorption rules, and none of them has been repeated on data it was not found on. The candlestick test (§15) set its rules first and kept 2024–2026 back as an unseen check. Every test and its exit rule is listed in §14.

Supported Partly Not supported Untested

The honest summary

No rule in this toolkit has survived a proper control. The one partly supported result — the value-area fill — is a small in-sample excess over a base rate, measured without a stop. The volume-shelf "lingering" effect that earlier versions of this guide leaned on disappeared once distance from the open was controlled (§9). Treat everything here as a structured way to take and record sim trades, not as a proven method.

Two facts about this feed shape everything below:

  • XAUUSD here is a CFD. There is no real traded volume and no buy/sell flag on trades. "Volume" is a tick count, and buy-versus-sell pressure (delta) is estimated. Measured: that estimate explains only 1.7% of a bar's price move. Treat anything built on delta as a weak hint.
  • On this feed a volume profile is close to a time profile. Volume is a tick count, so a "high-volume" price is mostly a price where the market spent time. Yesterday's shelves do get revisited more than yesterday's gaps — but by exactly as much as any level at the same distance from today's open. Measured: no volume effect (−0.2 bars, §9).

Which HVN set should absorption use?

Each heatmap now publishes its own levels per timeframe, so running it on H1, M5 and M1 at once is safe. On the M5 absorption indicator, InpHvnSource chooses which set filters the bubbles:

  • M5 (the default) — today's session shelves. Busier, more signals.
  • H1 — the multi-day shelves. Fewer signals, at levels more traders can see. Needs the H1 heatmap chart to stay open.
  • Never a lower timeframe than the chart. The indicator warns you if you try.
Do not raise InpLookbackBars

OF_Absorption re-reads the ticks of every lookback bar on each new bar. At the default 400 bars on M5 the terminal already logged "indicator is too slow, 5,734 ms". A few thousand bars would stall MT5 for tens of seconds every bar.

Section 5

Pre-session routine

About fifteen minutes, done at 21:15 before the New York open. The order matters: each step narrows the next.

  1. Check the status linesTop-left of each chart. Any red line (HVN STALE, PROFILE WARMING, CSV error) means that chart is not telling you the truth yet. Fix it or ignore that tool tonight — §10 explains each one.
  2. Read H1: trend or rotation?SMC swing structure: a recent BOS in one direction = trend; alternating CHoCHs = rotation. Note whether price sits in the premium (top 5%), discount (bottom 5%) or the middle of the swing.
  3. Write down the H1 shelvesThe labelled HVN prices on the H1 heatmap, plus the previous day's high and low. They are reference prices for tonight — candidate targets, not tested ones (see §9).
  4. Locate price against M5 valueInside the value area = expect rotation, fades are allowed. Above VAH = buyers in control, no shorts into strength. Below VAL = sellers in control, no longs into weakness.
  5. Mark where the Asian and London ranges endedFrom the session boxes. The Asian high/low is the classic place London and New York run stops.
  6. Write your maximum loss for the nightOn paper, in dollars, before the first trade. Three losers ends the session regardless.

Section 6

Reading each layer

Heatmap cells

Every coloured rectangle behind the candles is one bar wide and one price row tall. The row height is the bucket size — 10 points, or $0.10, on gold with the recommended settings. The colour says one thing only: how much volume traded in that price row during that bar, measured against the busiest rows on the chart.

Cell colourRelative volumeWhat it means
dark red, faintjust above zeroPrice traded here, but barely. Thin.
bright redup to 40%Light trade.
orange40–75%Busy.
yellow → white75–100%The heaviest trade on the chart. This is a shelf.
no colour — chart backgroundnoneNo trade at that price in that bar at all.
Red is the quiet end, and no colour means selling

The default Fire scheme runs dark red → red → orange → yellow → white as volume rises. So a red cell behind a candle is near the thin end of the scale, not the busy one — the levels that matter are the orange and white shelves. Cells also fade as they thin out, so quiet rows are both dark and see-through.

Nothing in the colour carries direction. There is no buy/sell, bullish/bearish or delta meaning in a red or a white cell: this is traded volume at a price, full stop. Aggressor side on a gold CFD feed is inferred rather than real — run OF_Probe once to see what your feed actually carries. If red keeps reading as "sell" to your eye, switch InpScheme to Ice (blue) or Mono (grey).

Two settings shape the picture. Log scale (on by default) lifts the quiet rows so they are visible at all — but it also means the colour bands are spaced by orders of magnitude, so a mid-orange cell holds a small fraction of the busiest cell's volume, not half of it. Saturate at 99% makes the busiest 1% of cells all render at full heat, so one freak bar cannot flatten the rest of the chart to black.

Reading it: a long candle with one bright shelf and a lot of dark space is not a strong move — price passed through most of that range without trade. The shelf is the level; the empty part of the wick is noise.

Is the volume real? The first status line at the top-left answers it: green means real exchange volume, orange means tick counts — the number of price updates, which is a decent proxy for activity but not contracts traded. HVN bands and LVN lines No effect under control

The thin gold bands with an "HVN price" tag, and the grey dotted LVN lines between them. They show where yesterday's and today's trade concentrated. The tested claim was that price lingers at shelves and crosses gaps fast. The raw numbers looked like it (25 vs 15 bars), but the same gap appears for levels with no volume meaning at the same distance from the open (21 vs 12). The volume shelf itself added nothing.

What that leaves: shelves are a readable picture of where the market spent time, useful for orientation and for naming a level. Any rule of the form "targets at HVNs, stops beyond LVNs" is untested craft, not evidence.

POC and value area Partly

The POC (red) is the single most-traded price, and the value area (blue dashes) holds 70% of the session's volume. The POC is a sensible reference and realistic target, but it is not a magnet: the next session trades through the prior POC 66.7% of the time, against 66.2% for a mirrored level the same distance from the open. The value-area fill below is the one place value has a measured edge.

SMC structure Untested

  • BOS — a close through the last swing in the trend's direction: trend continues.
  • CHoCH — the first close through a swing against the trend: the warning shot. On M1 this is the cleanest objective trigger in this toolkit.
  • Order blocks and FVGs — see-through boxes. Treat them as areas of interest. They matter most where they overlap a volume shelf; an order block sitting in an empty LVN had little trade behind it.
  • Premium / discount — the top and bottom 5% of the active swing. Longs from discount and shorts from premium have more room to run.

Absorption bubbles Not supported as a trigger

Unusual volume, little movement, close rejecting one end. Every tested threshold gave a negative average, and results got worse as criteria tightened — the signature of noise. Use a bubble only as a tiebreaker: if you already have a level and a rejection, a bubble at that level is mild supporting evidence. A bubble on its own is not a trade.

Session boxes

A thin, low-volume Asian range is not real participation. When London or New York trades beyond the Asian high or low and then closes back inside, stops were run. That is context for the sweep setup in §8, which is untested.

Section 7

From context to entry

The same sequence for every trade. If a step fails, stop there — do not skip ahead because the chart "looks good".

  1. Context agrees (H1)Your trade direction fits the H1 picture: with the trend, or a fade inside a rotation. A long from the H1 premium zone in a downtrend fails this step.
  2. A named level (M5)You can say the exact price and what it is: an H1 HVN band edge, the prior value-area high or low, the Asian high or low, or an order block that overlaps a volume shelf. "Somewhere around here" is not a level.
  3. Price comes to youSet an MT5 price alert a few dollars before the level and wait. Entering early because you expect the level to hold is the most common way to take a full-size loss.
  4. Rejection on M1At the level, an M1 bar closes back on your side of it, and the M1 internal structure prints a CHoCH in your direction. An absorption bubble at the level strengthens it; its absence does not cancel it.
  5. Risk check before the clickStop beyond the rejection extreme. Target at the next named level (placement by HVN/LVN is untested — §9). Reward at least 2× risk. Lot size calculated (§11). If the target is closer than 2R, there is no trade.
  6. Manage by the mapTake part of the position at the POC or the first HVN on the way. Do not move the stop to breakeven until that first target fills. Flat by 04:00 whatever happens.
This sequence is not Setup A's method

Step 4's M1 CHoCH trigger is untested. Setup A — the only partly supported setup — does not use it: its measured trigger is six consecutive M5 closes inside the prior value area, with no M1 step. The sequence above describes setups B, C and D; take Setup A exactly as its card is written.

No-trade conditions

  • Any red status line on the chart you are trading from.
  • Price is sitting in the middle of an HVN band — it will chop.
  • Within 15 minutes of a scheduled US release (CPI, NFP, FOMC). Spread widens and levels stop meaning much.
  • The first 30 minutes after the 05:00 profile reset (PROFILE WARMING) — the session profile is built on almost nothing.
  • Three losing trades already tonight.

Section 8

The setups

Four patterns, each written the same way so you can compare them. Only the first has a measured edge; the rest are there to be proven or dropped in sim.

A · Value-area fill

Partly · +8.4 percentage points over base rate
Market
The session opens outside the prior day's value area.
Where
The value-area edge price re-enters through.
Trigger
Price re-enters value and holds: six consecutive M5 closes between VAL and VAH (30 minutes).
Entry
Market, on the close of the sixth bar inside.
Stop
Not part of the test. A reasonable starting point: back outside the edge you came through. Whatever you choose changes the hit rate below.
Target
The opposite value-area edge — the only target that was measured.
Invalid if
An M5 bar closes back outside the edge you entered through. Not part of the test either.
Evidence
Measured as a yes/no event with no stop and no time exit: after the sixth inside close, did price touch the opposite value-area edge at any point before the server day ended? It did in 239 of 545 setups (43.9%), on 545 of 1,833 session pairs. The comparison is 35.4% of all sessions covering the whole prior value area — an unconditional rate, not a matched control. This is a touch rate, not a win rate: it does not tell you how often a trade with any particular stop wins, and no per-trade R or expectancy exists for it. In-sample only.

B · LVN traverse

Untested · premise failed its control
Market
Price is leaving an HVN band towards an LVN, in the direction H1 agrees with.
Where
The edge of the HVN band facing the gap.
Trigger
An M5 bar closes clear of the band edge, moving into the LVN. On M1, the first pullback that fails to re-enter the band.
Entry
Market on the M1 failed pullback (or the M5 close if you cannot watch M1).
Stop
Back inside the band it just left. If price returns to the shelf, the move has failed.
Target
The near edge of the next HVN band beyond the gap. Never inside the gap.
Invalid if
An M5 bar closes back inside the band.
Evidence
The idea rests on price crossing LVNs faster than it leaves HVNs. Once distance from the open was controlled, that difference disappeared (§9). The entry has never been tested. Keep it only if your own sim log shows something.

C · Shelf rejection with confluence

Not supported mechanically · sim only
Market
H1 is rotating, or price is at the edge of the H1 swing (premium for shorts, discount for longs).
Where
An H1 HVN band or prior value edge that overlaps an SMC order block or fair value gap. The overlap is the point: structure with trade behind it.
Trigger
On M1: price pushes into the zone, a bar closes back out, and internal structure prints a CHoCH away from the level. An absorption bubble at the level is a tiebreaker.
Entry
Market on the CHoCH close, or a limit back at the level after it.
Stop
Beyond the rejection extreme and past the nearest LVN — not inside the shelf.
Target
The POC, then the far value edge or the next H1 HVN.
Invalid if
An M5 close through the far side of the zone.
Evidence
Tested as: enter at the level on the first touch of the prior day's VAH or VAL, exit at the close 12 M5 bars (1 hour) later, no stop, no target. Fading VAH averaged −57 points (−$0.57 per ounce) gross. Fading VAL averaged +72 points gross, but 5 of 7 years were flat or negative after a 36-point spread, and 2026 alone carried it. Confluence and the M1 trigger are the untested filters that would have to rescue it.

D · Asian range sweep

Untested

Where
The Asian high (for shorts) or low (for longs), drawn by OF_Sessions.
Trigger
Price breaks the Asian extreme, then an M5 bar closes back inside the Asian range, with an M1 CHoCH back inside.

Beyond the sweep's extreme.

Section 9

Stops and targets

No tested support for this table

Earlier versions called this the toolkit's strongest part, citing the 1.7× lingering result. That result used levels from the prior session and counted bars in the next one, so it was not circular. But it never controlled for distance from the open. Rerun with each level mirrored across the open: HVN 25 vs mirrored 21 bars, LVN 15 vs mirrored 12, and a paired volume-attributable difference of −0.2 bars over 1,828 session pairs. Within distance bins HVN and LVN are the same (e.g. 68 vs 67 bars within $2 of the open). The test also used one HVN (the smoothed profile maximum) and one LVN per day, not the indicator's multi-node levels.

The table below is conventional practice, kept as a starting point for your sim log. It is untested.

  • Mind the spread. It was 36 points ($0.36) when measured. On a $3 stop that is 12% of your risk before price moves. Stops tighter than about $1.80 (five spreads) are mostly paying the broker.
  • Aim for the near edge of a band, not its centre. Price starts stalling as it reaches the shelf.
  • Never widen a stop once the trade is on. If the level is wrong, the trade is wrong.

Section 10

Status-line warnings

The dark panels at the top-left of each chart tell you whether an indicator can be trusted right now. Orange and red are not decoration — each one below means something specific.

HVN STALE - SIGNALS BLOCKED

The heatmap for the chosen HVN timeframe was removed or has not refreshed for over 5 minutes, so absorption is suppressing bubbles. Open that timeframe's heatmap chart again, or change InpHvnSource.

HVN ABSENT - SIGNALS BLOCKED

Usually just after the 05:00 reset while the profile warms up, or the heatmap found no nodes. Expected; wait it out. If it persists, check the heatmap is on the right timeframe.

HVN STALE - FILTER BYPASSED

Pass mode is on: bubbles are showing without the HVN check. Do not treat them as HVN-confirmed.

LEGACY v1.00 globals

An old heatmap version is on the chart. Remove it and add OF_Heatmap again.

PROFILE WARMING (12/30)

Fewer than 30 bars in the session scope. HVNs are not published yet on purpose — a three-bar profile is noise.

PROFILE TRUNCATED

The scope is bigger than 2,000 bars; the profile covers the most recent bars only. Normal on a long Rolling scope.

RECENCY OFF - SCOPE TOO LARGE

You turned on recency weighting, but it would re-read too many bars of ticks and freeze MT5, so it was switched off.

rolling fallback on 37/400 bars

Those bars did not have enough same-time-of-day history, so their volume Z-score uses the older flat window. Session-open bubbles on those bars are less reliable. More history helps: Tools → Options → Charts → Max bars in chart.

BAR data (no ticks)

The heatmap could not get ticks and built the picture from bars. Normal on H1; on M5 it means tick history has not downloaded yet.

CSV SCHEMA MISMATCH

The existing CSV file has different columns. Rename or move the old file (File → Open Data Folder → MQL5 → Files); nothing is written until you do.

CSV WRITE PENDING (2 rows)

The file is locked, usually open in Excel. Close it; the rows are kept and written on the next bar.

Contract facts for this XAUUSD, read from the terminal: 100 oz per lot, so 1.00 lot moves $100 for every $1 in gold and 0.01 lot moves $1. Minimum lot and lot step are both 0.01.

lots = risk in dollars ÷ (stop distance in dollars × 100)

Tuning to two or three signals a day

  1. Read the firing rate on status line 1: "x.x/day over N days" and "last 24h".
  2. On attach, the Experts tab prints p50 / p90 / p95 / p99 for delta ratio, price impact and range/ATR, and the percentage of bars clearing each current threshold. Move the threshold that your method actually uses — Z-score and impact for the Impact method.

  5. After 100+ CSV rows, send the file over and it can be analysed properly: outcome by session, by HVN filter, by stacked, by threshold.

Section 13

Risk rules and the gate

Rules for every session

  • 1% of the account per trade, at most.
  • Three losers and you stop for the night. Close the platform.
  • Flat by 04:00 Malaysia time. Never hold while asleep.
  • At least 1:2 reward-to-risk. At the measured hit rates, less than that loses by arithmetic.
  • No level, no trade.

Say these six out loud before clicking

  • Which exact level am I trading against, and what is it?
  • Is price inside, above or below value — and does my trade agree?
  • Where exactly is my stop, and what price would prove the idea wrong?
  • Where is my target, and is it a level I can name (for Setup A: the opposite value edge)?
  • Is the reward at least twice the risk?
  • What is my lot size, calculated?
The gate before real money

100 sim trades, each journaled against its written setup. Positive expectancy after costs, spread included. 95% rule-following — a winning trade that broke the rules counts as a failure for this purpose. Until all three are met, everything here stays in sim.

Section 15

Candlestick entries

This section teaches you to read candles and to spot the four patterns most often taught as entries — and shows what happened when each one was traded mechanically on your gold feed. Learn to read them; do not trade them on their own.

Tested before this was written — none pass

Eight pattern rules (four patterns, each in both directions) were fixed in advance and run on XAUUSD M5, with 2024–2026 kept back as an unseen check. Before costs every one averaged about 0R — the same as entering on a random bar. After the $0.36 spread every one lost, from −0.06R to −0.37R per trade, in both periods. The full table is below.

Reading one candle

A candle is a summary of one fight between buyers and sellers over a fixed time — five minutes on M5. Four prices build it, and the most informative is where the close sits inside the range.

Body
Open to close. Green when close is above open, red when below. A big body means one side controlled the whole period.
Wicks
Prices reached but not held. A long lower wick means sellers pushed down and were pushed back.
Range
High to low. Compare it with the last 14 candles (ATR) — a pattern made of tiny candles means almost nothing.
Close location
(close − low) ÷ range. Near 1 = buyers finished in control; near 0 = sellers did.

The four patterns, exactly as tested

Each card gives the precise rule, where the stop goes, and the measured result: average R per trade after the spread, on the 2019–2023 data and on the held-back 2024–2026 data.

Engulfing

Bullish: after a decline, a red candle is followed by a green candle that opens at or below the red close and closes at or above the red open, with a bigger body, and a range of at least half the ATR. Bearish is the mirror after an advance.

Meant to show: the side in control got completely overturned in one bar.

Stop: beyond the more extreme of the two candles.

2019–23 · bull / bear−0.35R / −0.33R
2024–26 · bull / bear−0.15R / −0.18R

Hammer / shooting star

Hammer: after a decline, lower wick at least 60% of the range, body at most 30%, upper wick at most 15%, range at least 0.8 × ATR. Shooting star is the mirror after an advance — a long upper wick.

Meant to show: one side drove price hard and was rejected before the bar closed.

Stop: beyond the wick's end.

2019–23 · hammer / star−0.37R / −0.35R
2024–26 · hammer / star−0.19R / −0.24R

Inside-bar breakout

A candle sits completely inside the previous candle's range (the "mother"). The next candle closes beyond the mother's high (long) or low (short) — a wick poking through does not count.

Meant to show: a pause, then a decision.

Stop: the other side of the mother candle.

2019–23 · up / down−0.30R / −0.32R
2024–26 · up / down−0.06R / −0.16R

Morning / evening star

Morning star: a long red candle (body at least 60% of its range, range at least 1 ATR), then a small candle (body at most 30% of its range, range at most 60% of the first), then a green candle closing above the first candle's body midpoint. Evening star is the mirror.

Meant to show: momentum, exhaustion, then reversal over three bars.

Stop: beyond the extreme of all three candles.

Why they fail on this chart

  • No direction in the pattern. Gross results sit within a few hundredths of 0R, like the random-bar baseline. With a 2R target you break even at about 33% wins, and that is roughly what every pattern — and a coin — delivers.
  • The spread is large next to an M5 stop. The net figures are 0.15–0.35R below gross, which means $0.36 is roughly a fifth to a third of the typical pattern stop. The patterns look "less bad" than the random-bar baseline after costs because their stops span two or three candles, so the spread is a smaller share of R — not because they predict better (gross is the same).
  • They are common. Engulfing candles appear about 12 times a day in each direction. Something that frequent cannot carry much information.
  • Net results are closer to zero in 2024–26 while gross results are not better, so the difference comes from the cost side: the same $0.36 is a smaller share of each stop. Gold's wider 2024–26 ranges are the likely reason; stop sizes by period were not separately measured.

How to spot them live anyway

Reading candles well is still a real skill: it tells you who won the last few minutes and keeps you from entering in the middle of a move. If you use a pattern, use it only to time a trade you already have a reason for — at a named level from §8 — and treat that combination as untested until your sim log says otherwise.

  1. Wait for the closeA pattern does not exist until the candle closes. A hammer at 4 minutes 30 seconds can close as a plain red bar.
  2. Check the sizeThe pattern candle's range should be at least the recent average (ATR). Small candles are noise.
  3. Check what came beforeA reversal pattern needs something to reverse: a clear move into it over the last five or so candles.
  4. Check the locationIs it at a level you named before the session? If not, it is one of a dozen similar candles today — skip it.
  5. Check the costStop distance at least $1.80 (five spreads), so the spread is at most 0.2R. If the pattern's natural stop is tighter, do not tighten further — skip the trade.
  6. Enter on the next candleMarket at the next candle's open, stop beyond the pattern extreme, target at 2R or the next named level. Log it in the journal as "candle-timed".

Mistakes that make it worse

  • Seeing the pattern on a still-forming candle and entering early.
  • Moving the stop inside the pattern to make the position size bigger.
  • Counting a wick through a level as a breakout — the test used closes.
  • Reading M1 candles for patterns: the spread is an even larger part of an M1 stop.
  • Remembering the patterns that worked and forgetting the ones that did not. Log every one you would have taken.

Practice drill

  1. Open the Strategy Tester (Ctrl+R), pick any EA or indicator, set XAUUSD M5, tick Visual mode, and choose a past week.
  2. Play at a slow speed. Each time you see one of the four patterns, pause and write down: time, pattern, direction, whether it was at a named level, entry, stop, 2R target.
  3. Resume and record what happened.
  4. After 50 patterns, compare the ones at a named level with the ones that were not. That comparison is the untested question worth your time.
How this was tested

XAUUSD M5 on this feed, 6 Aug 2019 – 11 Sep 2026. Rules above written into the script before it ran. Entry at the open of the candle after the pattern; stop beyond the pattern extreme (skipped if under $0.50); exit at +2R, at the stop, or at the close of the 48th candle (4 hours). If a candle touched both stop and target it counted as a stop. Cost: $0.36 per trade. Pass rule set in advance: positive net R with t > 2 in both 2019–2023 and 2024–2026, and above a random-bar baseline (60,000 random candles, random direction, stop at that candle's own low or high). Eight rules tested; all eight failed in both periods. The fixed $0.36 may overstate cost in the cheaper early years, but gross results are near zero regardless.
